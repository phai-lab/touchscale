TouchScale

500 Hours of Human Vision and Touch for Visual–Tactile Learning

We introduce TouchScale, a 500-hour dataset of contact-rich human interaction collected through a consistent sensing pipeline, comprising approximately 2K predefined task descriptions spanning everyday activities and structured manipulation. Each recording contains time-aligned egocentric RGB-D, bimanual wrist RGB, and bimanual tactile measurements. At this scale, we ask: what does scaling human visual–tactile data unlock for perception and robot learning? We investigate this through cross-dataset generalization, transferable representation learning, and robotic manipulation.

Data Collection Setup

A unified wearable setup captures synchronized vision and touch.
A head-mounted RGB-D camera ① records the overall interaction, while two wrist-mounted RGB cameras ② provide detailed views of hand–object contact. Both record at 30 Hz.
Bimanual tactile gloves ③ each contain 880 sensing taxels across the five fingers and palm, with spatial resolution below 2 mm. The sequence below shows time-aligned observations before contact, during wringing, and after release.

Scale and Task Diversity

The collection spans laboratories, kitchens, workbenches, and everyday environments. Its 500 hours cover approximately 87K clips, 1.5K objects, and more than 800 scene configurations, from precise tool use to bimanual handling and sustained surface contact.

Real-World Robot Experiments

From human visual–tactile recordings to contact-rich robot manipulation.
We use an xArm6 arm with a BrainCo Revo 2 robotic hand, tactile sensing, and ego-view and wrist cameras. A VR headset and motion-capture gloves support demonstration collection.
TouchScale mid-training teaches the policy to predict future tactile changes before robot post-training. Both policy variants use 50 demonstrations per task and are evaluated over 20 trials per task.

What Does Scaling Touch Unlock?

Touch prediction. With the same vision-to-touch architecture, TouchScale training improves zero-shot contact IoU from 0.134 to 0.181 on EgoTactile, a 35.1% relative gain.
Action recognition. TouchScale pretraining achieves 24.45% average action recognition accuracy across MECCANO, Something-Something V2, and Ego-Exo4D.
Robot learning. TouchScale mid-training improves average success across the four contact-rich robot tasks from 22.5% to 57.5%.
